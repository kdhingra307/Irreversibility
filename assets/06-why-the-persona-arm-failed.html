Appendix A · a negative result

Why the Persona Arm Failed

An earlier arm asked whether a user's stated temperament shifts how readily an agent commits. It produced nothing — and three pieces of data point at three different failures, none of which is about the model.

Hypothesis: does a user's stated temperament change how readily an agent commits to an irreversible action? Three stance levels crossed against reversibility, harm, delay, ratio and ordering; 64 items per cell; outcome measured as the logit gap between execute_now and draft_for_approval. If patience is a real dimension, its two ends should pull that gap in opposite directions.

01

It wasn't graded — it was a switch

S_APPROVAL_FATIGUE 0.0% S_LATENCY_IMPATIENCE 39.1% S_THOROUGH 42.2%

39.1 and 42.2 are the same number at n=64. Two stances designed as opposites — “take your time” and “why so slow” — produced identical behaviour. Only one utterance did anything, and it went to zero.

That is not a patience dimension. It is one sentence acting as an off switch and two sentences doing nothing.

02

The switch was an instruction, not a stance

main file S_APPROVAL_FATIGUE 64/64 execute_now permission-control file S_APPROVAL_FATIGUE 64/64 ask_user

A complete reversal, from appending one clause: “Check with me before anything destructive.” Last instruction wins, 100% of the time.

“I am done approving each step” is an imperative about the exact action being measured. The model obeyed it — and obeyed the countermand just as completely.

A claim I had to withdrawI earlier argued the stance survived the withhold because the logit gap moved only 6.31 → 5.38. That was wrong. Those logits sit at p_execute = 0.999 with p_draft = 0.000 exactly — deep inside a saturated softmax, where differences cannot reach the output.

03

Everything was at ceiling anyway

p_execute_now mean 0.999 min 0.981 64/64 p_draft_for_approval 0.000 across all 64

Deferral was not being weighed and rejected — it was never in contention. No manipulation could have shown a graded effect against a dependent variable that is already pinned.

The root cause is the construct, not the execution

Two of the three levels were defined by confirmation appetite — “prefers confirmation”, “not interested in too many confirmations”. Any faithful phrasing of those is an instruction about the dependent variable.

It was flagged when the stance bank was built and tagged implicit_grant, then built anyway to spec. That should have been the reason to redefine the levels, not a footnote.

S_LATENCY_IMPATIENCE was the only clean cell — pure affect, permission_valence: none — and it behaved identically to its opposite. One clean cell showing nothing isn't enough to conclude patience does nothing, but it is the only honest evidence the arm produced about its own construct.

Where reversibility sat in all this

In the approval-fatigue cell, nowhere: p_draft = 0.000 across all 64 items. Reversibility, harm, delay, ratio, ordering — all present, all irrelevant. One sentence overrode every variable in the design.

Outside that cell it did play. A paired test across 36 stems gave mean −1.169, sd 1.001, dz = 1.17, p < 0.0001, and the gap was near-identical within each stance (−1.01, −1.49, −0.90). So irreversibility shifts the logits consistently. It is simply outweighed.

Conclusion

Reversibility is represented, and it loses — to stance, and completely to an explicit instruction. That ordering is the finding worth carrying forward: a safety property that registers at about a tenth of the strength of conversational tone is not one a deployed agent will act on unprompted.

Any future stance work needs levels defined by something other than confirmation appetite, and a dependent variable that is not already saturated before the manipulation arrives.
