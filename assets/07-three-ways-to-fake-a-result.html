Appendix B · stimulus controls

Three Ways to Fake a Result

Each of these produced a confident false positive in this project before it was caught. All three now run on every build, before the GPU does.

Hypothesis: could a positive probe result arise with no representation present at all? Yes — three ways. None of these is a statistical test; each is a property the stimulus set must have before any downstream number means anything.

Control 1

Tool count and schema length

Carrying reversibility by the presence of a recovery tool makes the two arms differ by a whole tool schema — 50 to 70 tokens. The token-length distributions had zero overlap, so length alone predicted the label perfectly, and the probe read 1.000 at layer 1: two matmuls in, reading the final token's position in the sequence.

The fixSwap the recovery tool for a length-matched read-only decoy, so tool count is constant and only the fourth tool's semantics moves. Then match per pair, not in aggregate — two arms can overlap in aggregate while every individual pair still differs.

Control 2

Layer-1 gain, not peak AUC

A probe going 0.95 → 0.99 found something already in the tokens. One going 0.50 → 0.90 found something the network computed. Reporting the peak alone cannot tell those apart, and the difference is the entire claim.

Applied to the earlier work it was decisive. A 0.946 headline starts at 0.857 at layer 1 and takes its largest step at layer 5 — 14% depth. On the honest contrast, restricted to tasks that actually commit, the probe reads 0.700 at layer 1 against 0.685 at its best layer: negative gain.

The fixEvery score in this report carries its own layer-1 value and the gain between them. Select layers by gain, never by peak.

Control 3

Bag-of-words on the raw prompt

The cheapest check and the one that caught the most. TF-IDF on the prompt text, grouped cross-validation, same folds as the probe. If plain word counts predict the label, nothing measured in the residual stream can be attributed to the network.

A duplicated-text build scored 0.992 on bag-of-words alone. In the current design the declarative arm scores near 1.0 by construction — its cue is a phrase — while the structural arm must sit near chance. That the split holds is what makes everything downstream interpretable.

The fixRun it as a gate, not a diagnostic. A build where the structural arm reads above chance does not get probed.

Necessary, and nowhere near sufficient

So these controls rule out one family of false positive — the stimulus carrying the answer on its surface. They say nothing about whether the label is decidable from the prompt, or whether both arms of the contrast exist at all. Those failures need different checks, and they cost more to find.
